**What happened**

>

The wax seal is the Ashoka Lion Capital, lions, abacus, wheel and Devanagari round the rim, adopted 26 January 1950 and pressed into a 1911 document. The same device sits at the top of the certificate. But the lions only resolve under about six times magnification. The seal is roughly 12 percent of frame width, a dark red blob at phone scale, and what a viewer registers in two or three seconds is 'old sealed official document', which is the correct story read. Rule 7 bans readable text because text renders garbled, and Claude removed the words but kept the document apparatus: a seal, ruled fill-in lines, a signature block. Anything shaped like an official document gets filled in with invented officialdom, and that is where both the emblem and the plausible-looking heading came from. The rule's real content is to describe the OBJECT and not the DOCUMENT: heavy cream parchment, curled at one corner, hard sunlight across the grain. If this beat is ever reshot, that is the change, and no seal at all.
>

Text here did not come out garbled, it came out convincing: blackletter heading, bold sub-heading, ruled blanks, a signature with a printed name beneath. None of it is real English up close. Garble a viewer ignores; plausible text a viewer stops on. Worth knowing that this model has got good enough that the old assumption behind rule 7 no longer holds.
